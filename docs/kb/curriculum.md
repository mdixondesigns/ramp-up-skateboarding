# Curriculum

What actually happens in a class. This is the most persuasive material the
business has, because "week 3 is the day your kid drops in on a ramp" beats any
adjective. Use it.

## Free clinic

One session. Same lesson plan as day 1 of Foundations, more or less.

Covers standing, getting on and off the board safely, standing on it properly,
pushing for some speed and cruising, and stopping. Then small obstacles: a
limbo stick to go under, and a curved speed bump to ride over.

The goal is to get past the common early frustrations and leave a kid with
enough to ride around their neighborhood and practice.

Kids who did a free clinic and then enroll in Foundations simply do day 1
again. It is good practice and there is very little pushback, because this time
around they are the big fish.

## Foundations, day by day

One hour per class.

**Day 1. Get rolling.**
Start with the wheels immobilized using Skater Trainers so nothing rolls away.
Getting on and off the board. Standing properly. Then pushing, and cruising to
get comfortable. Finish with the limbo stick and the speed bump.

**Day 2. Turning.**
Two types: carving and kick turns. Both practiced through games and activities.

**Day 3. The drop-in.**
Dropping in on a quarter pipe that Mike brings. It is a small 12-inch ramp.

**Day 4. Rails.**
Beginner-friendly rails that do not require an ollie. Kids ride
in a straight line over them. Mostly about speed and aim.

**Day 5. Getting air.**
Going fast off the kicker so it launches them and all four wheels leave the
ground.

**Day 6. Build your own skatepark.**
Kids choose from all the obstacles used across the session and combine them
creatively into their own park, then skate it.

**Days 7 and 8** (8-week programs only) are extensions: practice and review of
anything above.

### How this maps to program length

**The first three days are the crucial ones** and are always the same: on-board
basics, turning, and the drop-in.

For a **4-week program**, the last day is chosen from rails, air, or
build-your-own, depending on the cohort. Do not promise all three on a 4-week
program.

Longer programs get through more of the list and get more practice time.

### End state

By the last day of a multi-week Foundations program, kids can push, turn, and
stop confidently. They look out for each other and understand the basics of
skatepark etiquette. They have felt what it is like to ride over very small
rails, get a little air off a kicker, drop in on a small quarter pipe, carve,
and do kick turns.

The outcome to lead with: **they are ready to go have fun safely at the
skatepark.**

## Intermediate

Still structured, but not an ordered path. No two Intermediate classes have the
same activities. They all share the same shape, which is the thing worth
selling: a kid always knows what is coming next.

1. **Word of the week** (5 min). One idea, discussed. Examples: risk, grit,
   park etiquette, goals, self-discipline.
2. **Warm up.** Sometimes laps, sometimes back and forth, sometimes a simple
   game.
3. **Fundamental skills.** Things that can be practiced at home. **The ollie
   lives here.**
4. **Park skills.** Things that can be practiced at a skatepark.
5. **Free skate.**
6. **Recap** (2 min).

### The ollie

The goal of Intermediate. It takes **between 4 and 24 weeks**, depending
greatly on the kid. That honest range is better copy than a promise.

Because it can take multiple seasons, the class structure above is what makes
each season feel complete on its own. Every class delivers a word, a warm up,
new skills, and free skate whether or not this is the week the ollie lands.

### Boards at Intermediate

Ramp Up provides boards at both levels, so nobody is ever stuck. But
Intermediate kids should get their own board to practice at home, the same way
a kid learning an instrument needs one at home. Link to the buying guide in
Parent Resources rather than recommending specific products.
