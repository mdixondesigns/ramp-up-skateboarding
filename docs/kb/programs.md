# Programs and the level ladder

What we sell and how a kid moves through it. For what actually happens in a
class, see `curriculum.md`. For prices, see `pricing.md`.

## The ladder

```
Free clinic  ->  Foundations  ->  Intermediate  ->  (repeat until they ollie)
```

**Free clinic.** One session, free, no experience and no gear needed. The top
of the funnel and not revenue. Lesson plan is essentially the same as day 1 of
Foundations.

**Foundations** (beginner). Ages 5 to 9. All kids skate together.
Goals: pushing, turning, stopping, basic park etiquette, building confidence,
and making friends.

**Intermediate** (level 2). Ages 6 to 12. Some activities together, some split.
Grouped more by skill than by age.
Goal: the ollie.

**Level 3** exists in theory and **has never been run.** Do not put it on the
site.

## Public names

"Foundations" and "Intermediate" are the real, public, parent-facing names.
Use them. "Level 1" and "level 2" are internal shorthand.

## Program shape

- One hour per week
- 4 to 8 weeks long
- Length is driven by **partner scheduling requirements**, not by different
  outcomes. A 4-week and an 8-week Foundations cover the same material. Longer
  programs mean more practice time.

## How kids move up

After Foundations, there are two options and neither is wrong:

**Run Foundations again.** Good for anyone under 6, and good for anyone who
wants more time on the basics. It is the kid and parent's choice. Coaches are
happy to make a recommendation. Kids have done Foundations three times and
gotten better every round.

**Move up to Intermediate.** Kids typically stay in Intermediate for multiple
seasons until they can ollie. That is the design, not a delay.

### Readiness rubric

Approved for publication.

- Can comfortably **push, turn, and stop by themselves** → eligible for
  Intermediate.
- Can **drop in, hit the rail, or launch the kicker by themselves** → should
  definitely be in Intermediate.
- Under 6 → another round of Foundations is usually the better call regardless
  of skill.

### Entry into Intermediate without Foundations

Allowed, but not advertised as a standing offer. In practice it happens when a
kid has a friend already in the class, when a kid is clearly already skilled,
or after a private lesson used to assess them. Mike's judgment overrides any
published rubric.

### `[proposed]` The five-minute skills check

Not yet tested in the field. Drafted as a parent-facing self-check so families
can decide without gatekeeping, done in a driveway with a sneaker for a cone:

1. Push and roll down the driveway without stepping off
2. Stop on command, under control, not by jumping off
3. Turn one way by carving, then the other
4. Turn one way by kick turning.

All four unassisted means ready for Intermediate. First two but not the turns
means one more round of Foundations. Unsure means come to a free clinic and ask
a coach.

The coach version adds a fifth item a parent cannot test at home: **drop in on
the twelve-inch quarter pipe, unassisted.** Five tasks at roughly ninety
seconds each fits inside ten minutes.

## After Intermediate

`[proposed]` Once kids can ollie, the plan is invite-only skate clubs: smaller,
semi-private, more student-directed toward the kid's own goals. **This has
never run. Do not put it on the site.**

## Private lessons

A real revenue line, currently four sentences on the site. Best fit is a kid
who hung back at a clinic, or a kid progressing faster than their group.

Birthday parties and private group events are **off the site for now.** Ramp Up
has no permanent space, which makes them hard to coordinate. Possibly someday.

## Site structure

Decided: three pages.

- `/programs` — an index listing the 2 or 3 programs currently open, with price
  and level on each. This is the page that changes every season.
- `/programs/foundations` — evergreen explanation of the level.
- `/programs/intermediate` — evergreen explanation of the level.

The split exists so seasonal edits never touch copy that is already right, and
so each level page can rank for its own search terms.
