# Programs and the level ladder

What we sell and how a kid moves through it. For what actually happens in a
class, see `curriculum.md`. For prices, see `pricing.md`.

## The ladder

```
Free clinic  ->  Foundations  ->  Intermediate  ->  (repeat until they ollie)
```

**Free clinic.** One session, free, no experience and no board needed. Families
bring a helmet, which is required at every session. The top of the funnel and
not revenue. Lesson plan is essentially the same as day 1 of
Foundations.

**Foundations** (beginner). Ages 5 to 9. All kids skate together.
Goals: pushing, turning, stopping, basic park etiquette, building confidence,
and making friends.

**Intermediate** (level 2). Ages 6 to 12. Some activities together, some split.
Grouped more by skill than by age.
Goal: the ollie.

**Level 3** exists in theory and **has never been run.** Do not put it on the
site.

## Public names

"Foundations" and "Intermediate" are the real, public, parent-facing names.
Use them. "Level 1" and "level 2" are internal shorthand.

## Program shape

- One hour per week
- 4 to 8 weeks long
- Seasons: **spring, fall, and winter.** There are no summer sessions. See
  below, because the site currently explains this nowhere.

### Summer

**Do not explain why.** The skateboarding season is simply three quarters. A
customer does not need the reasons behind it, and giving them invites a worry
that was not there.

What a June visitor should get instead, close to Mike's own words:

> Our season runs three quarters of the year. Want to keep practicing over the
> summer? Hit up your local skatepark. We might see you there.

That last line is the Foundations promise in action rather than a gap in it.
The entire point of the program is a kid who can go enjoy a skatepark on their
own, so pointing them at one is the product working.

Right now a June visitor gets an empty calendar and concludes Ramp Up is
defunct. Say the season plainly, say when it starts again, and offer the email
list.

**Internal only, not for the site:** multi-week sessions do not fill in summer
because family schedules scatter. Mike and Christine both work full time year
round, so summer camps are not currently possible, and the break is used to
work on the business. Summer camps are a future offering gated on having enough
certified instructors. Do not put them on the site until they exist.
- Length is driven by **partner scheduling requirements**, not by different
  outcomes. A 4-week and an 8-week Foundations cover the same material. Longer
  programs mean more practice time.

## How kids move up

After Foundations, there are two options and neither is wrong:

**Run Foundations again.** Good for anyone under 6, and good for anyone who
wants more time on the basics. It is the kid and parent's choice. Coaches are
happy to make a recommendation. Kids have done Foundations three times and
gotten better every round.

**Move up to Intermediate.** Kids typically stay in Intermediate for multiple
seasons until they can ollie. That is the design, not a delay.

### Readiness rubric

Approved for publication.

- Can comfortably **push, turn, and stop by themselves** → eligible for
  Intermediate.
- Can **drop in, hit the rail, or launch the kicker by themselves** → should
  definitely be in Intermediate.
- Under 6 → another round of Foundations is usually the better call regardless
  of skill.

### Entry into Intermediate without Foundations

Allowed, but not advertised as a standing offer. In practice it happens when a
kid has a friend already in the class, when a kid is clearly already skilled,
or after a private lesson used to assess them. Mike's judgment overrides any
published rubric.

### `[proposed]` The five-minute skills check

Not yet tested in the field. Drafted as a parent-facing self-check so families
can decide without gatekeeping, done in a driveway with a sneaker for a cone:

1. Push and roll down the driveway and back without stepping off
2. Stop on command, under control, not by jumping off
3. Turn around a cone one way, then the other
4. Lift the front wheels and swing the board around

All four unassisted means ready for Intermediate. First two but not the turns
means one more round of Foundations. Unsure means come to a free clinic and ask
a coach.

The coach version adds a fifth item a parent cannot test at home: **drop in on
the twelve-inch quarter pipe, unassisted.** Five tasks at roughly ninety
seconds each fits inside ten minutes.

Two conditions if this is ever used: never call it a test in front of the kid,
run it as a warm-up or a game, and publishing it does not remove Mike's
discretion to wave a kid in.

## Private lessons

**Real, not theoretical.** A few dozen have run over the years.

### How they work today

- Organized manually over email
- Held either at a skatepark, before or after a group session, or at the
  client's house
- Hour-long sessions for students aged 6 to 14

Shorter sessions for under-5s do exist, but **Ramp Up is not catering to that
age group and it stays off the site entirely.** See `content-rules.md`. The
site's age range is 5 to 14, with no exceptions mentioned.

### Where this is going

Ramp Up is **changing the model.** The goal is to move one-on-one lessons off
Mike and onto USSEA-certified instructors, then build a proper booking page
where instructors post their own availability and rates and parents book
directly through the site.

### What the site says in the meantime

**Keep it polite and vague.** Something like "contact us for details if you're
interested in private lessons." Do not build out a full private lessons page,
publish rates, or promise availability while the model is in flux.

The current page's "coming soon" framing is wrong, though, because these
already exist. "Get in touch" is the right shape, not "not yet."

### Birthday parties

Birthday parties and private group events are **off the site for now.** Ramp Up
has no permanent space, which makes them hard to coordinate. Possibly someday.

  and level on each. This is the page that changes every season.

so each level page can rank for its own search terms.
