# The team

Source material for `/about/team`, which is the page doing the most trust work
on the site. A parent reads it right before deciding to hand their child to an
adult they have never met.

### The line that anchors the page

In Mike's own words: **his core competency is youth enrichment education, not
skateboarding.** He describes his own skill level as very casual. He does not
push the limits, and he loves every second of it.

**Do not soften this, and do not bury it.** It is the most trust-building thing
available to the site, for the same reason "the success rate for
brick-and-mortar skate shops is criminally low" works on the History page.
Admitting something makes everything else believable.

It also answers the question every parent is actually asking, which is not "can
this guy skate" but "does this guy know how to teach my kid." Ten years of
youth education answers that. A trick list would not.

### Why skateboarding

Skateboarding is what Mike and his closest friends did in middle school and
high school. What he took from it: resilience, adaptability, empathy,
friendship, risk, grit, self-confidence, advocacy. Some of the best times of
his life. He thinks kids today would benefit from the same lessons.

Worth noticing: that list is almost exactly the "word of the week" list from
the Intermediate class structure in `curriculum.md`, and it overlaps the four
pillars on `/about/philosophy`. The personal story and the curriculum are
saying the same thing. Let them rhyme rather than writing them as separate
ideas.

## Coaching for Ramp Up

Source material for `/get-involved/coach-with-us`, which currently has two live
FAQ answers written as the literal string "[Answer]".

**Yes, it is paid, and the rate is publishable.** Assistant coaches start at
**$22.50 per hour**, including assistants under 18.

That is the only question a prospective coach genuinely cares about, and the
page currently ducks it. Put the number directly under the headline. It is a
strong rate for part-time youth coaching, and a candidate who has to email to
find out what it pays usually just doesn't email.

**Hours.** Sessions run 4 to 8 weeks, in spring, fall, and winter. A coach can
expect **3 to 9 hours per week** during a session.

**Certification.** Lead coaches must be USSEA certified. Assistant coaches do
not need it. See `operations.md` for what the certification covers.

**The skill bar is low, and saying so is the point.**

Anyone who can comfortably push, cruise, turn, and stop is ready to coach. That
is the whole requirement.

Why it works: **a coach is on the board for less than 10% of a class.** The
rest of the hour is spent on your feet, acting as a spotter, a mentor, and a
lifeguard for a group of kids.

**An enthusiastic, experienced teacher with zero skateboarding experience can
do this after 1 to 3 hours of skate training.**

That last line should be on the page in roughly those words. It changes who
applies. Most people assume a skate coach has to be a skater and rule
themselves out before they finish reading. The real hire is a good youth
educator, which is a far larger pool and a better fit for the job as described.

"Lifeguard" is the word that does the most work here. Keep it.

### A symmetry worth using

The coach skill bar, push, cruise, turn, and stop, is **exactly the same bar** a
kid has to clear to move from Foundations up to Intermediate. See
`programs.md`.

So a line like "if you can do what our eight-year-olds can do by the end of
Foundations, you can skate well enough to coach here" is true, disarming, and
funny. It also quietly tells a prospective coach how forgiving the standard is
without making it sound unserious.

### Recruiting is a growth lever, not an HR page

Two things Ramp Up wants to do are both gated on the same constraint, having
enough USSEA-certified instructors:

- **Summer camps**, which cannot run until there is instructor capacity
- **Private lessons at scale**, which are meant to move off Mike and onto
  certified instructors with their own booking availability

So `/get-involved/coach-with-us` is not a side page. It unlocks two revenue
lines. Write it with that weight.

## What the page needs to do

1. Answer "who is teaching my kid" with a name and a face.
2. Make the education background the headline, not the skateboarding.
3. Carry the credentials from `operations.md`: USSEA certification, First Aid
   and CPR, three PA clearances, Safe Sport training.
4. Give each coach one human detail. A day job, how long they have skated,
   something they are bad at. That is what makes a coach a person rather than a
   bullet point.
