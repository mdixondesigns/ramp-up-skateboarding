# Operations, safety, and logistics

The practical facts a parent or a hosting partner asks about.

## Group size

| Program type | Kids |
| :--- | :--- |
| Multi-week (Foundations, Intermediate) | 6 minimum, 12 maximum |
| Free clinics | up to 15 |

Free clinics run larger because show rates are lower for free programs.

## Coaching

- Ratio: **1 coach for every 6 kids.** An assistant coach steps in when a
  program has more than 6.
- **This is safe to state as a flat fact, not an aspiration.** Fewer than five
  classes out of 400 to 500 have run above the ratio. Write "one coach for
  every six kids," not "we aim for."
- **The same coach every week** for a multi-week program. This is a real
  selling point and it is currently written nowhere on the site.

### Two roles

**Lead coaches** must be USSEA certified. **Assistant coaches** do not need the
certification.

Assistant coaches can be as young as 14. Anyone under 18 is **always**
accompanied by a lead coach and is otherwise treated the same as an adult
assistant, including being paid.

### A solo coach is normal

A class of six or fewer kids is run by one lead coach, on their own. Most
parents stay and watch for the whole session, so other adults are usually
present, but they are parents rather than staff.

The Parent Resources FAQ used to say "there is never a session with a single
adult on site." That was false and has been removed. The honest version is
warmer anyway: a coach may be the only staff member at a small class, and you
are welcome to stay, most parents do.

### Clearances

Every coach and assistant coach **18 or older** carries all three Pennsylvania
clearances, and Ramp Up keeps them on file:

- PA Child Abuse History Clearance
- PA State Police criminal record check
- FBI fingerprint-based background check

Assistants under 18 do not carry these. They are always working alongside a
lead coach who does.

Publishable wording: *Every coach 18 and older carries all three Pennsylvania
clearances. Assistant coaches under 18 are always working alongside a lead
coach.* Do not write "all our coaches are background checked" without that
second sentence.

### Certification

Lead coaches are certified by **USSEA, the U.S. Skateboard Education
Association.** The certification is an online course followed by a live
assessment, and it includes:

- Red Cross First Aid and CPR
- Heads Up concussion training
- Safe Sport sexual abuse prevention training

This is the answer whenever the site says "certified." Name USSEA rather than
leaving the word to float.

## Where classes happen

Basketball courts, tennis courts, and quiet parking lots. Sometimes indoors.
There is no permanent Ramp Up space.

Note that we rarely teach Foundations at a skatepark, even though the outcome of
Foundations is being ready for one. That is deliberate and worth explaining: skateparks can be intimidating, dangerous, and distracting.

## Weather

If a class is rained out, Ramp Up makes its best effort to add another class to
the tail end of the program. **An extra week is typically built into the
schedule** to absorb cancellations.

This is a genuine differentiator. Most youth programs cancel and keep the
money. It deserves a callout, not a line buried in an FAQ.

## Minimum enrollment, postponement, and refunds

**A program needs at least 6 signups to run.**

If it is short, Ramp Up postpones, and does so **at least 3 days before the
first session** so nobody is left guessing on the day. If it cannot be
rescheduled, the program is cancelled.

For families already signed up:

- They are **automatically moved to the new dates.** Their spot is safe and
  they do not have to re-register.
- If the new dates or times do not work for them, they get a **full refund.**
- If the program is cancelled outright, they get a **full refund.**

This pairs with the rain policy above. Together they are a single reassuring
idea worth saying out loud on program pages: if we cannot run it, you are not
out the money and you do not have to chase us.

For partners, this is also the answer to "what if we only get eight signups."
Eight is fine. Six is the floor.

## Gear

- Ramp Up provides skateboards at every level, including Intermediate.
- 12-15 boards travel to a session.
- Helmets are required, every session.
- **Families supply helmets.** Ramp Up has a limited number of spare helmets available for when people forget. The back-ups are not advertised.
- **Ramp Up has no pads to lend.** Helmets are the only loaner safety gear.
- **Pads are recommended and are the parent's choice.** Ramp Up strongly
  recommends wrist guards for kids over 100 pounds. That recommendation comes
  from real incidents rather than generic caution. The incidents themselves are
  internal, see `content-rules.md`.
- **Why pads are not required:** the discomfort of wearing pads can bother a
  kid more than the risk being protected against. Ramp Up supports any family
  that wants their kid in pads and requires only a helmet.
- Kids bring a helmet, closed-toe sneakers, and a water bottle.

By the time kids reach Intermediate, most have their own board. See
`curriculum.md` for how to frame that.

### First aid

Because a solo staffer is always a lead coach, and every lead coach holds USSEA
certification with Red Cross First Aid and CPR, **every session has at least one
adult with current First Aid and CPR training.** The lead coach carries the
first aid kit.

## Insurance

Ramp Up carries commercial general liability insurance written specifically for
youth recreation programs. The policy includes **sexual abuse liability** and
assault and battery coverage, which many small youth programs do not carry.

Separately, Ramp Up carries a **blanket accident policy** covering every
registered participant during a program. It pays accident medical expenses up
to $25,000 per person per incident, over a $100 deductible, and it is
**secondary** to a family's own health insurance.

So the honest phrasing is "helps cover medical costs if your kid gets hurt,"
never "we cover it." Most youth programs carry nothing like this, and it is a
concrete answer to the worry underneath "is it safe."

A certificate of insurance is available to hosting partners on request.

**The certificate is not public facing.** Do not put it, or the carrier, agency,
policy number, or the insured address, on the website or in any public
document. Those details are deliberately not recorded in this repo either.

Coverage runs on an annual policy term, so re-verify before repeating any
insurance claim in a new season.

Publishable wording: *We carry commercial general liability insurance for youth
recreation programs, including sexual abuse liability. We're happy to provide a
certificate of insurance.*

## Hosting a program

What a partner needs to provide. There are very few hard requirements, and the
partner page should say so, because a rec director assumes there will be many.

### The space

**Ideal: a basketball court.** The flat surface and the size are both right.
Tennis courts, quiet parking lots, and indoor gymnasiums all work well.

**Smallest that works: half a basketball court**, roughly 2,000 square feet.
Not much less than that. Either phrasing is fine to publish, and both together
is better: the square footage is what a facilities person can check, the
basketball court is what everyone else can picture.

The only real requirements are common sense:

- Relatively smooth surface
- No live traffic
- Not surrounded by breakable things: drywall, glass cases, flower beds

A fence is **not** required. Restrooms are a plus but **not** required.

Ramp Up brings mobile barriers to slow down a loose board. They are pool
noodles strung on a cord, held down by plastic-covered weights. Concrete,
charming, and worth naming on the partner page rather than saying "safety
measures."

### Promotion

**Marketing is a shared responsibility, not a hard requirement.** Ramp Up
encourages partners to help fill the program, and the things that actually
help are:

- An email to their list
- A post on their social channels
- Engaging with Ramp Up's own posts
- Displaying flyers

**Ramp Up supplies the materials:** print-ready PDFs, the program description
and details, and a social graphic pack.

That pairing is the whole point, and the partner page should present it
together. "Help us promote" sounds like work. "We send you a flyer, a
description, and a post you can paste" takes five minutes. Say the second one.

### What Ramp Up brings

The full list, which is most of the partner page's job:

- 12 to 15 skateboards
- A USSEA-certified lead coach with First Aid, CPR, and all three PA clearances
- Assistant coaches as needed to hold the 1:6 ratio
- A first aid kit
- Mobile pool-noodle barriers to stop runaway boards
- Commercial general liability insurance including sexual abuse liability,
  with a certificate available on request
- Blanket accident coverage for every registered participant
- Registration handled through Sawyer, unless the partner runs their own
- The curriculum, and the same coach every week

### Lead time

Preferred, though none of it is hard and fast:

- **One month** to promote a free program
- **Two to three months** to promote a paid program

Planning in January for an April start is comfortable.

A free program running directly into a paid one also works well. For example,
one month promoting a free clinic, then a six-week paid program starting two
weeks after it.

That pattern is worth pitching to partners rather than waiting to be asked. The
free clinic fills the paid program, which is the whole funnel in one
conversation.

### Sharing a public space

When using part of a skatepark or another shared space, Ramp Up's preference is
to keep as much of it open to the public as possible. Worth publishing: it is a
good-neighbor policy and it preempts the objection that a program will take
over a facility.

## What a first session covers on safety

Stepping on and off the board is the first skill. Skate Trainers immobilize 
the wheels. Pushing and cruising comes next with an emphasise on a wide, stable stance.

## Registration

Sawyer is the registration platform. Most program pages carry a Sawyer embed.

For programs where Ramp Up does not handle registration (a partner takes
signups themselves, for example a township), the page carries **all the
comparable information a Sawyer listing would have shown** and then links out
to the external registration. These pages are the harder writing job, because
nothing downstream will answer a question the page leaves open.

## Scholarships

Scholarship spots sometimes available. Just ask. There are no forms.

Payment plans are sometimes available as well.

## Unknown

Insurance carrier and coverage amount, and host space requirements. See
`open-questions.md`. Do not guess at either, they are exactly the claims that
must be true.
