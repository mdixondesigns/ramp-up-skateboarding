# History

Source material for the About pages. The existing copy on `/about/history` is
in Christine and Mike's own voice and is some of the best writing on the site.
Preserve its tone.

## The origin

Christine and Mike had been married 11 years and in business together longer.
In 2022, looking to shake things up, Christine suggested they "quit our jobs and
open a skate shop in town." The motivation was to get more connected with their
community and take control of their own destiny.

A cursory investigation made it clear that the success rate for
brick-and-mortar skate shops is criminally low.

Around the same time, Mike was running a side project called PA Skateparks,
visiting and cataloguing 60+ skateparks across Pennsylvania. In the process he
noticed two things inside skateboarding that looked sustainable and even
growing: building concrete parks, and teaching kids. Neither of them knows
anything about construction. Youth programming was right in their wheelhouse.

The lightbulb moment was Christine's: maybe they didn't need a physical
skatepark to get started, at least not at first. What if they could prove the
concept out of the back of a van?

## What happened next

**Private lessons came first.** That was the initial test, and the response was
good enough to keep going.

**Then group sessions.** Ramp Up started reaching out to parks and recreation
departments to find who would be interested in hosting group programs. The
group sessions filled up quickly, and **classes have been nearly sold out ever
since.**

That last fact is a strong, unused proof point. It says demand without saying
anything boastful.

**Then the work of building a real program.** Across those years: figuring out
which equipment actually works for beginners, meticulously designing a pathway
that takes someone with no experience through skateboarding in an accessible
way, and adding layers that make the program safer and more fun.

This is the part that connects the origin story to `curriculum.md`. The
day-by-day Foundations sequence is not a lesson plan someone wrote in an
afternoon, it is four years of iteration. Say so.

**2024: level 2 launched**, now called Intermediate, and it became popular.

**2025: the family moved from Phoenixville, PA to Lancaster, PA.** Ramp Up has
been replicating the model there and has already taught over 200 kids in
Lancaster.

The move also explains something the site currently leaves confusing. The
Philadelphia-area testimonials are not from work that was dropped, they are
from where the business used to be based. Worth knowing when writing the About
pages, even though the decision stands to strip locations from the testimonials
themselves.

## Growth

| Year | Skaters served |
| :--- | :--- |
| 2023 | 90 |
| 2024 | 250+ |
| 2025 | 400+ |

Every one of them on a board Ramp Up brought.

These numbers currently live on `/about/history` in a StatBand.

**A second, unused proof point:** Ramp Up has run roughly 400 to 500 classes.
That counts classes rather than skaters, so it is a different claim from the
table above and it is written nowhere on the site. Worth confirming the figure
before publishing, since it came up in conversation as an estimate.

**A third:** over 200 kids taught in Lancaster since the 2025 move. That one is
useful specifically for local credibility, since a Lancaster parent reading
"400+ skaters" does not know whether any of them were nearby.
